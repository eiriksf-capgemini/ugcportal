## Executive summary

- **There are two ways to earn from UGC.** (1) **Paid UGC for brands**: you make photos or videos, and the brand posts them on its own channels. You don't need followers. (2) **Your own channel**: your gallery site plus social media, earning through affiliate links, sponsorships or ads. That route needs an audience and takes longer.
- **For a "bottle of wine a month" goal, both work.** Paid UGC brings money sooner. Your own site builds an asset that grows slowly. **Recommendation:** start with your own site and channels as a portfolio and hobby, add affiliate links for books, tech and food, and pitch Nordic brands directly for paid UGC once you have 5–10 good samples.
- **Wine is the biggest legal trap in Norway.** Norway bans all alcohol advertising in every medium, including blogs and social media. Writing about wine you bought yourself, as a private person, is generally fine. **Taking free bottles, payment, trips or affiliate commission connected to alcohol is not.** Since September 2024, authorities can fine people who break this rule on social media.
- **All paid or gifted content must be clearly labelled** "Reklame" or "Annonse" under Forbrukertilsynet's guidance. This includes affiliate links, discount codes and free products.
- **Tax:** a true hobby with no profit is tax-free. Once you aim to make a profit over time, it counts as a business: income is taxable, **free products count as income at market value**, and you need to keep accounts. VAT registration is required if turnover passes NOK 50,000 in 12 months (unlikely at your scale).
- **The large US UGC marketplaces (Billo, JoinBrands) do not accept Norway-based creators.** Plan to pitch Nordic brands directly or use global platforms like Insense, after checking that they accept Norwegian creators.

## 1. What "UGC" means, and the models open to you

| Model | How you earn | Followers needed? | Time to first income | Fit for you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Paid UGC for brands** | A brand pays per video or photo set and posts it on its own channels and ads | No | Weeks (once you have a portfolio) | Good for books, food, tech. **Not wine** (see §3) |
| **B. Your own site and channels** | Affiliate commission, sponsored posts, ads, later digital products | Yes, it grows slowly | Months to a year | Good for all niches; wine only as a non-commercial hobby |
| **C. Hosting other people's uploads** | Community or traffic → ads or affiliate | Yes | Long | Not recommended to start: brings moderation, privacy and copyright duties |

**Analysis:** Your gallery site is a natural **portfolio** for Model A and a **home base** for Model B. Model C (opening uploads to visitors) brings legal duties (GDPR, copyright takedowns, and the EU Digital Services Act, which Norway expected to apply from 2026) that are out of proportion to a hobby budget.

### Typical paid UGC rates (international market data, not Norway-specific)

- Beginners: around **USD 50–250 per piece** (or **GBP 50–150 per video**). Some datasets put the average for creators with fewer than 50 projects at **USD 200–400**.
- One or two small jobs a month would cover your wine budget.
- Equipment to start: a smartphone, ring light, small tripod and clip-on microphone, roughly **USD 150** in total.

## 2. Getting started: a practical sequence

1. **Set up accounts in your wife's name.** Affiliate networks, platform profiles, contracts and the payout bank account should all be in the same name, so records stay consistent if you revisit tax later.
2. **Choose a lead niche and a name.** Books, food and tech can go together under one theme, for example "slow evenings: what we read, cook and use". Keep wine as editorial content inside that theme, not as a product category you earn from.
3. **Turn the gallery into a portfolio.** Add an "About us" page, a "Work with us" page and a contact form. Show 5–10 sample pieces in different formats: unboxing, short review, how-to, recipe walkthrough, "flat lay" photo set.
4. **Make spec samples with products you already own.** For example: a book review reel, a recipe video, a tech-gadget "first impressions" piece. Never fake a partnership. Don't imply that a brand hired you.
5. **Start affiliate links where it's allowed** (books, tech, cookware, later outdoor gear). Nordic networks include **Adtraction** (200+ Norwegian programmes, including bookshops such as ARK), **Awin**, **Partner-Ads**, **Tradedoubler** and **Adrecord**.
6. **Pitch brands directly.** Small Nordic food producers, publishers, kitchenware and gadget brands are realistic first clients. Send a short email with your portfolio link, one specific content idea and a price.
7. **Set up the paperwork as soon as money or free products come in:** a simple income and expense log, a contract template (scope, usage rights, duration, price), and a labelling routine.
8. **Review after 3–6 months.** What took the least effort for the most return? Expand into hiking and camping once you have a routine.

## 3. Do's and don'ts

### 3.1 Wine and alcohol (most important)

**Facts (Helsedirektoratet, alkoholloven § 9-2):**

- Norway has banned **all alcohol advertising** since 1975. The ban is media-neutral, so it covers blogs, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and websites.
- "Advertising" is read very broadly: any mass communication intended to promote sales. A product doesn't have to be named. Phrases that clearly suggest alcohol, such as "en kald en" or "happy hour", can be enough.
- **Private people and independent media are generally exempt.** The condition is that the content is not initiated, paid for or rewarded by anyone in the alcohol industry.
- **Sponsoring bloggers with products, trips or events** is given as a typical example of getting around the ban, and is not allowed.
- **Links** to blog reviews of specific products can count as advertising when they are made to promote sales.
- Since **13 September 2024**, illegal alcohol advertising on social media can be met with an **administrative fine**.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Write about wine you bought yourself, from your own point of view, as a hobby | Accept free bottles, samples, tastings, trips or event invitations from producers, importers, Vinmonopolet suppliers or restaurants in return for coverage |
| Focus on food pairing, regions, culture and your own tasting notes in a neutral tone | Use affiliate links, discount codes or paid placement for alcohol, including foreign online shops |
| Keep wine content apart from your paid partnerships | Put alcohol in sponsored content for *other* products, e.g. a wine glass in a paid cookware video |
| Consider **alcohol-free alternatives** (allowed if sold under a separate brand from the alcoholic product) | Use "drink-positive" visuals in commercial content: toasting, people drinking, frosty bottles |
| Ask Helsedirektoratet if you're unsure about a specific case | Assume a disclaimer or an English-language post gets you out of Norwegian law |

**Analysis:** Once your site earns money in other categories, an authority could argue it is no longer purely private. To lower the risk, make sure wine content **never** carries affiliate links or ads, and never comes from gifts.

### 3.1a Wine accessories: glasses, coolers and apps (your chosen angle)

Accessories are not alcohol, so you can promote and earn from them. Two parts of § 9-2 still apply:

- **Alcohol must not appear in advertising for other products.** Helsedirektoratet's examples include pictures that give a clear association with alcohol, *whatever the glass actually contains*.
- **Products sharing a brand or trademark with an alcoholic drink** are covered by the ban.

| Product | Risk | ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wine glasses** | 🟢 Low | Show **empty** glasses: design, shape, how they feel in the hand, dishwasher test, storage, comparing glass shapes | Fill glasses with red or white "wine-looking" liquid. No pouring, toasting, swirling or people drinking. Don't name wines or regions as "perfect for this glass" in sponsored posts. Avoid glasses carrying a winery's or drinks brand's logo. |
| **Wine coolers / wine fridges** | 🟢 Low–medium | Focus on noise, energy use, temperature zones, size, installation, build quality. Show the cooler empty, or with generic unlabelled bottles at most. | Shelves full of identifiable labels. Lines like "a cold one is always ready". Brands that also sell wine. |
| **Wine apps** (cellar logs, tasting notes, label scanners, pairing guides) | 🟠 **Medium–high** | Only apps that are **purely tools**: cellar inventory, your own tasting notes, a temperature or storage guide. Review features and usability. | Sponsored or affiliate content for apps that **sell wine**, show prices or deals, push specific products, or are run by importers, retailers or producers. Promoting an app like that is very likely promoting alcohol sales. Avoid screenshots showing specific wines with ratings or prices in paid posts. |

**Practical rules for this angle:**

1. **Before any paid deal, check who's behind the brand.** If the company also produces, imports or sells alcohol, decline.
2. **Keep commercial and personal wine content visually apart.** A sponsored glass review shouldn't sit next to "our Friday Barolo".
3. **Wine apps are the grey zone.** If a specific app collaboration is tempting, ask Helsedirektoratet first. They answer questions about the advertising ban.
4. **Labelling still applies.** A sponsored glass or cooler post is "Reklame" like any other.

### 3.2 Labelling advertising (Forbrukertilsynet)

**Facts:**

- Any post where you receive **payment or another benefit** must be clearly labelled as advertising. Benefits include free or loaned products, discounts, trips and events.
- The label must be **prominent**, easy to read and come **first**. The recommended words are **"Reklame"**, **"Annonse"** or, for links, **"Annonselenke" / "Reklamelenke"**.
- **Words Forbrukertilsynet says *not* to use:** "sponset", "i samarbeid med", "affiliatelenke", "gifted", "gave", "ambassadør", "invitert".
- **Blog posts with affiliate links:** label the whole post at the top *and* label each link.
- **Discount codes count as advertising** and need the same labelling.
- Both you and the brand are responsible.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Put "Reklame" at the start of the caption, video overlay or post title | Hide the label in hashtags or at the end of the text |
| Label free review copies (books!) and loaned tech | Assume a free book "doesn't count" |
| Keep a short labelling checklist and use it every time | Rely on platform "paid partnership" tags alone without checking they meet the Norwegian standard |

### 3.3 Tax and accounts (Skatteetaten)

**Facts:**

- **Hobby** (not economic, no profit): income is **not taxable**, and you get **no deductions**.
- **Business** (*næringsvirksomhet*): the activity is (1) likely to make a profit, (2) meant to last, (3) of some scope, and (4) at your own risk. Then income is taxable, expenses are deductible, and you must keep accounts.
- **There is no fixed amount** that marks the line between hobby and business. Earning something "every month" is a sign of a business.
- **Free or discounted products are taxable income** at market value. This covers books, gadgets, kitchenware and so on.
- **Affiliate income** counts the same way as other income.
- Products you didn't ask for and throw away: record them in an internal voucher (date, item, signature) to avoid tax.
- **VAT:** registration is required once turnover passes **NOK 50,000** in 12 months.
- You can ask Skatteetaten for a **guiding statement** (*veiledende uttalelse*) if you're unsure.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Keep a simple log from day one (cash and products received, value, date), even as a hobby, so the hobby/business assessment is easy to revisit | Treat free products as "not income" once it becomes a business |
| Keep a simple spreadsheet: date, source, cash, value of products, expenses | Mix hobby and business without records |
| Keep receipts for equipment (camera, lighting, props) | Forget that income is taxed by where you run the site from, not where the server is |

**Applied to your set-up:**

- Skatteetaten looks at the *nature* of the activity, not only how much it earns. Regular affiliate income and paid jobs, even small ones, are signs of a business.
- **Practical trigger for revisiting:** repeated paid jobs, affiliate payouts every month, or products of real value coming in regularly.
- If it becomes a business, it is taxed for the person running it (your wife, as owner), even though you share income at home.

> If you're unsure how to set this up for your situation, talk to Skatteetaten or an accountant. This document doesn't replace that.

### 3.4 Your website: privacy and cookies (GDPR and the new ekomlov)

**Facts:**

- From **1 January 2025**, Norway's new Electronic Communications Act (*ekomloven* § 3-15) requires **GDPR-level consent** for non-essential cookies, including analytics, affiliate tracking and ad pixels.
- Consent must be **active**: no pre-ticked boxes, no "by using this site you accept", no relying on browser settings.
- Datatilsynet and Nkom share oversight.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Use a proper consent banner with "Accept" and "Reject" buttons of equal weight | Load Google Analytics or affiliate scripts before consent |
| Publish a short privacy policy (what you collect, why, how long you keep it, contact) | Collect email addresses for a newsletter without clear consent |
| Prefer privacy-friendly analytics that don't need cookies, where possible | Add visitor upload or comment features without thinking about moderation and personal data |

> ⚠️ **Data protection note:** if you later collect personal data (newsletter, comments, user uploads), GDPR applies in full. Use Datatilsynet's guidance and get privacy advice before launch.

### 3.5 Copyright and image rights

**Facts and analysis:**

- **You own what you create.** Photos and videos you make are yours. When you sell UGC, define the **usage rights** in writing: which channels, paid ads or organic posts only, how long, and whether exclusive. Charge extra for paid-ad use and long licences.
- **Never use images from the internet** without a licence or permission. Crediting the source is not enough.
- **Book covers are a grey area.** Cover art and photos are protected works. Norway's quotation and illustration exceptions are narrow, and some don't apply in digital form. Safer options: the publisher's press images (ask the publisher), your own photo where the book is part of a wider scene, or written permission.
- **Recipes:** an ingredient list isn't protected, but the wording of the method and the photos are. Write recipes in your own words, and credit the source when you adapt one.
- **People in your photos and videos** need to agree, especially if the material is used commercially. Be careful with children.
- **Music in videos:** use the platform's licensed library or royalty-free music. Commercial UGC often needs music cleared for commercial use.

### 3.6 Niche-specific notes

| Niche | Opportunities | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Books** | Publisher review copies, bookshop affiliates (e.g. ARK via Adtraction), "bookstagram" or "booktok" formats | Label review copies as "Reklame". Cover-image rights. Spoilers. |
| **Food** | Recipe videos, kitchenware UGC, small Nordic producers, meal-kit brands | Health or nutrition claims in sponsored content are regulated (EU claims rules). Food safety in demos. Alcohol in shots (§3.1). |
| **Wine and drinks** | **Accessories: glasses, coolers, tool-type apps** (see §3.1a). Alcohol-free brands. Coffee, tea, soft drinks. | No alcohol in commercial imagery. No apps that sell wine. No gifts, links or sponsorship for alcohol. |
| **Technology** | Unboxings, how-tos, accessory UGC, electronics retailers on Nordic affiliate networks. Wine apps fit here too. | Loaned products count as a benefit and need labels. Be honest about weaknesses. |
| **Hiking and camping (later)** | Outdoor-gear retailers on affiliate networks, seasonal content, Norway's landscape is a strong visual asset | Allemannsretten and safety messages. Drones (Luftfartstilsynet rules). People and private property in shots. |

### 3.7 Platform and business practice

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check that a platform accepts Norway-based creators before investing time. **Billo and JoinBrands currently do not.** Insense is global, but confirm. | Pay upfront fees to "UGC agencies" or for "guaranteed brand deals" |
| Use a simple contract: deliverables, revisions, deadline, price, usage rights, payment terms | Hand over raw footage or unlimited rights for a beginner's price |
| Give honest opinions, including negatives | Post fake reviews or invent customer experiences (unlawful under marketing law) |
| Label AI-generated or heavily edited images where platforms or brands require it | Present AI-generated "tests" as real hands-on reviews |
| Keep batch-production days (shoot 4–6 pieces in one session) | Let it become a chore. You both said it's meant to be fun. |

## 5. Getting started: content, portfolio and being found

### 5.1 Content themes (first 2–3 months)

| Theme | Example content |
|---|---|
| **Books** | What we're reading, cosy reading corners, seasonal book lists |
| **Food** | Simple recipes, kitchen tools |
| **Wine accessories and tech** | Glasses (empty), wine coolers, gadgets you already own |

### 5.2 First six portfolio pieces (2–3 weekends, products you already own)

| # | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short vertical video (15–30 sec) | "3 books for dark autumn evenings" |
| 2 | Short vertical video | 30-second recipe, from start to finished plate |
| 3 | Photo set (5–8 images) | Flat-lay: book, coffee, reading lamp |
| 4 | Product review video | "Honest first impressions" of a kitchen or tech item |
| 5 | Photo set | Wine glasses: shapes, details, light through the glass (**empty**) |
| 6 | How-to | "How we set up our wine cooler": temperature, layout, noise |

**Quality basics:**

- Daylight from a window.
- Phone and tripod.
- A clip-on mic (sound matters more than picture).
- A tidy background.
- Hands and product in frame. Faces are optional.

### 5.3 Portfolio page on your website

- **Intro:** 2–3 sentences on who you are, what you make and your themes.
- **Your 6 pieces,** each with a one-line caption (format and length).
- **What you offer:** short videos, photo sets, reviews.
- **Contact:** email or form.
- **Later:** a one-page rate card.

### 5.4 Account set-up

| Platform | Set-up steps | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Switch to a **professional (creator) account**. Write a clear English bio covering your themes and a link to your portfolio page. Use the same name as your website. | Access to insights. Brands look for creators here. |
| **Pinterest** | Create a **business account** and **claim your website**. Make boards per theme: *Books & Reading*, *Simple Recipes*, *Wine Glasses & Accessories*, *Home Tech*. | Pins link straight to your site and keep bringing visitors for months or years. Claiming the site gives you analytics. |
| **Website** | Register in **Google Search Console** and submit a sitemap. Add a cookie banner if you use analytics. | Free data on how people find you. |

### 5.5 Being found (discoverability)

**Website (SEO):**

- One topic per page or post, with a descriptive title, e.g. "Simple Mushroom Risotto for Weeknights", not "Dinner #4".
- Descriptive **alt text** on every image.
- A short text with each gallery item, because search engines can't "see" photos.
- Write in natural English and use the phrases people actually search for.

**Pinterest:**

- Vertical images (2:3).
- Keyword-rich pin titles and descriptions, e.g. "Elegant wine glass shapes explained".
- Link every pin to the matching page on your site.
- Pin regularly rather than in bursts.

**Instagram:**

- Reels for reach, carousels for saves.
- A few relevant hashtags (around 3–5) rather than 30.
- Write captions in English.
- Reply to comments.
- Engage with accounts in your niches.

**Shoot once, post many times:** one session can give you a reel, a carousel, a blog post and 3–4 pins.

### 5.6 Weekly rhythm

| Day | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Saturday | Shoot 2–3 pieces | 1–2 hours |
| Sunday | Edit, write captions, schedule | 1–2 hours |
| Weekdays | Post 2–3 times a week. Reply to comments. | 10–15 min/day |

### 5.7 English-language notes

- **Norwegian law still applies.** You run the site from Norway, so the alcohol ban and labelling rules apply even when you post in English.
- **Labelling:** the label must be clear to your audience. Using **"Ad / Reklame"** (or "Advertisement / Annonse") covers both English-speaking and Norwegian followers. Use the platform's paid-partnership tool as well, not instead.
- **Affiliate networks:** Nordic programmes (Adtraction etc.) suit a Nordic audience. For an international English-speaking audience, consider international networks such as Awin as you grow.
